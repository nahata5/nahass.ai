About

Physician. Informaticist. Builder.

I'm Tom Nahass — a board-certified intensivist, Assistant Professor of Medicine at Rutgers Robert Wood Johnson Medical School, and Vice President of Health Informatics for the Central Region at RWJBarnabas Health.

Full CV Download PDF

I take care of critically ill patients, and I build the systems that decide what the people caring for them see. Those two jobs are usually held by different people. Holding both is the whole point — an alert designed by someone who has never been paged by it is a different object than one designed by someone who has.

At RWJBarnabas Health I lead health informatics across the Central Region of a twelve-hospital system: clinical decision support, AI deployment and governance, documentation integrity, quality programs, and operational analytics. The work I'm proudest of is the boring middle — the part between a model that performs well in validation and a nurse who actually does something different at 3 a.m.

How I got here

I studied music composition at Wake Forest. Composition is structure under constraint: you're given a small set of materials and asked to make something that holds together and moves people. I've never found that training irrelevant.

Medicine came after — a post-baccalaureate at Harvard Extension, then medical school at Rutgers RWJMS. Along the way I co-founded a digital health company, won a couple of Nicholson Foundation innovation prizes, and did freelance data science to keep the lights on. Internal medicine residency at Rutgers, then critical care fellowship at Memorial Sloan Kettering, where I built ICU management tooling because the tools I wanted didn't exist. An MS in health and clinical informatics from OHSU made formal what I'd been doing informally since 2012.

What I work on now

Early warning systems and what happens after the warning. Critical care digital twins. Large language models in clinical medicine — agentic systems and retrieval over real clinical data, with the evaluation and governance that has to come with them. I'm a co-investigator on a Rutgers BMIHAI-funded project on deep phenotyping of heart failure, and a co-mentor on an AI/ML digital-twin ICU fellowship.

Our deterioration-index and AI-triggered rapid response work across twelve hospitals was published in NEJM AI, with an 18% reduction in risk-adjusted mortality. That result is the argument for everything else on this site: the model was the easy part.

Outside the hospital

I'm in New Jersey with my family. I still write music. I'm an Eagle Scout, which surprises approximately nobody who has watched me plan a rollout.
